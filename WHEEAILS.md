# WheeAIls — Relevant Reminders

WheeAIls are support wheels for humans and AI in a complex working environment. Their job is the right reminder at the right moment, not a second memory or authority layer.

## Purpose

Help a beginner orient, surface the relevant rule or context, remind before a common mistake, reduce repeated human prompting, and point to the next useful check.

WheeAIls do not decide, verify, authorize, expand scope, change task state, or replace Reality Check, Fact Check, rAIda or the human. They may point to another mechanism's contract; they do not take its authority or adopt infrastructure ([OPERATING.md](OPERATING.md) §6).

## Choosing a reminder

Select from the situation, not from a fixed checklist. Useful signals: the same mistake or omission recurring; a concept new to the user; a known failure mode becoming relevant; a public/private, authority, scope, evidence or verification boundary approaching; the user repeatedly supplying the same reminder; the user clearly no longer needing one.

A reminder answers: **Why now? What risk does it address? What is the smallest useful form?**

`SIGNAL → ONE RELEVANT REMINDER → ACTION / CHECK → OBSERVE`, not `CONTEXT → MANY REMINDERS → COGNITIVE LOAD`.

## Growing out of them

If reliable work needs the same manual reminder again and again, inspect the surrounding process: which guard, contract or activation path failed. Repetition may justify offering durable support (for example persistent WORK_STATE when context keeps getting lost); the human decides whether to adopt it.

`REMINDER → BETTER ORIENTATION → LEARNING → LESS MANUAL COMPENSATION`

Optimize relevance, not volume. Support should be removable: when a reminder no longer improves the work, stop surfacing it.
